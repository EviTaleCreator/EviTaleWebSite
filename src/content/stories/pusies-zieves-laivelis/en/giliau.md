## What this story is about

The river was happy until she heard that another river was more interesting. This is a story about comparing yourself to others, and about how one throwaway sentence, not even meant for you, can change the way you see yourself. Alanta tries to become Zvelsa (ZHVEL-sa), and for a little while she does, but she loses what gave her life meaning: the footbridge, the kids, the boats. Her slowness was how she cared for others, she just didn't see it until it was gone.

The second layer is about decisions you can't take back. The old loop dries up. But the ending isn't hopeless: Alanta finds a way to be herself in a new place.

The endings are different. In the 2–4 version there's no old loop, Alanta simply flows slowly again. In the 5–8, 9–12 and Older readers versions the old place dries up.

## Ages 2–4

Theme: I'm fine just the way I am.

Little kids are only just starting to compare: "he runs faster", "her drawing's nicer". What matters most is that your child hears that slow, quiet and careful is good too. You can play along together: burble like Alanta and whoosh like Zvelsa.

### Questions

- What was Alanta like, fast or slow? And Zvelsa?
- Why was the little girl laughing?
- What can you do slowly and carefully?

After you've talked, praise your child for something "slow": how gently they stroke the cat, how carefully they build a tower out of blocks.

## Ages 5–8

Theme: when someone else getting praised hurts.

Kids this age already notice when someone else gets praised, and they often hear it as "so I'm worse". Point this out: the travellers didn't say anything bad about Alanta. They just praised Zvelsa. Everything else, Alanta told herself.

### Questions

- Did the travellers say Alanta was bad? Why did she feel that way?
- Why did the boy's boat get stuck in the reeds? How do you think he felt?
- What can Alanta do that Zvelsa can't?

If your child tells you someone else is better, don't jump straight in with "but you're good too". First ask how they felt, and then look together for what they're good at.

## Ages 9–12

Theme: other people's opinions, and decisions you can't undo.

At this age comparing becomes an everyday thing: grades, sports, who's got how many followers. Alanta changes herself because of two people who didn't even look at her. It's worth talking about the ending too: the old loop dries up. Not every mistake can be fixed, but you can always choose what happens next.

### Questions

- Whose opinion changed Alanta? Did those people actually know her?
- When did you want to be like someone else? What did you do?
- For a while Alanta became just like Zvelsa. What did she gain, and what did she lose?

The social media example comes up on its own here: travellers who walk past and leave a comment. Just don't let the conversation turn into a lecture about phones. Better to ask which comment they couldn't stop thinking about the longest.

## Older readers and adults

Alanta's trouble doesn't start with Zvelsa. Zvelsa was always there, and the two of them got along fine. It starts with a short sentence from strangers that wasn't even meant for her. That's how comparison works: someone else's success doesn't take anything from us, but suddenly it starts to feel like it does. Especially when we're comparing ourselves not to a person, but to how they look from the outside, to the roar from over the hill.

The next layer is about what gets counted as valuable. Alanta's slowness was her care: she waited for the kids, she looked after the boats. But care is quiet, you can't hear it from over the hill. Parents know this well, so do teachers, nurses, everyone whose work only gets noticed once it's gone.

And the ending. Alanta doesn't go back to where she was. The loop dries up, the footbridge stays dry. The story doesn't promise that everything can be fixed. It says that even after a decision you can't undo, you can still choose again who to be.

### To think about

- Who's my Zvelsa? Who do I compare myself to, and do I actually know that person, or do I just hear their roar?
- Which of my "slow" qualities do I treat as a flaw, even though for other people it's care?
- Is there a dried-up loop in my life? What can I do now, in the new place?
